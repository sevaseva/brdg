★ The Moonlight Hiking Almanac

_□×

The mountain is open all night. The trail, however, is much friendlier with a moon.

This page is unrelated to Bridge and is here strictly for fun HOWEVER all data on it is actually real and — let's hike at night!

Below are the good night hiking events in Cobb for the next two weeks, according to the club's volunteer almanac. A night makes the list if at least one of these is true:

  • A big moon is above the horizon in the evening, or a moon more than half full rises after sunset. It does not have to be full, but it has to be past half.
  • The bright Milky Way core (the Sagittarius end) is well up in the evening sky and the moon is down.
  • A major meteor shower is at its peak, with the meteors coming from high enough in the sky and no overwhelming moon.
  • A bright satellite (the ISS or Tiangong) crosses high over Cobb in the evening.
  • An eclipse is visible from Cobb. A solar eclipse is a daytime event, but the club still counts it as a reason to plan the day around one.
  • Two planets pass close enough together to be worth a look.

Planets, moon-washed meteors, and other interesting sights are noted on the cards even when they are not the reason a night made the list. All times are Cobb local time (Pacific).

How the Club Counts a Good Night

_□×

This is a very scientific almanac, according to the two club members who own a telescope and the one who owns a clipboard.

Sunset
The moment the top of the sun drops below the open horizon, allowing for the way the atmosphere bends light.
Sun behind the ridge
The sun does not always reach the open horizon: from this spot it drops behind Cobb Mountain first. This is the time it disappears behind the local ridge, worked out from a terrain profile of the surrounding hills. Trees, weather, and the exact spot you stand can still change it.
Big moon
Any night within four days either side of the full moon, because that is when the moon is still doing its best work.
Above the horizon
The middle of the moon is at or just above the horizon (−0.25°), which is about when the top edge clears it. A little more height means the moon is safely clear of trees, ridges, or the neighbor's ambitious flagpole.
Moonrise
The moment the top of the moon appears above the horizon. If it rises after sunset and the moon is more than half full, the night qualifies even if the moon is not especially big.
Milky Way core
The Sagittarius end is the part most people mean by the Milky Way. The club calls the core good while it is within 5° of its highest point of the evening, at least 10° above the horizon, and the moon is down, for at least twenty minutes. From Cobb the core tops out around 22°, so its height changes slowly near the top: half an hour either side costs well under a degree, an hour about 1.5°, and two hours about 5°, which is roughly 15% dimmer from the extra atmosphere — not something most eyes will notice in the dark.
Other reasons
Meteor showers count within one night of their peak when the meteors appear from at least 20° above the horizon and the moon is set or below half. A bright satellite pass counts when the ISS or Tiangong reaches 45° or more above the horizon in the evening. A visible lunar or solar eclipse counts, and so does a pair of planets within 3° of each other. The bar is deliberately high enough that the almanac still means something.

The chart is calculated for the club's viewpoint above Cobb (38.8253° N, 122.7025° W, about 987 m above sea level). Its open-sky times are usually within a minute or two of the U.S. Naval Observatory. Ridges, trees, weather, and the odd bear can still change your actual view.

Trail Notes

  • Bring a headlamp anyway. The moon is a mood, not a trail map.
  • Tell someone where you are going. This is the one piece of advice the club takes seriously.
  • Check closures and private land. A bright moon does not grant access.
  • Turn around while you still have energy. The downhill part is a lie told by the map.
  • No moon is not a failure. It is a good excuse for cocoa.

See you under the moon!

Walk quietly, bring a friend, and leave the trail as you found it. The moon has enough dust.
