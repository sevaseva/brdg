★ The Moonlight Hiking Almanac

_□×

The mountain is open all night. The trail, however, is much friendlier with a moon.

Below are the next seven good night hiking events in Cobb, according to the club's volunteer lunar almanac. A night makes the list if either:

  • A big moon is above the horizon at some point between sunset and the club's evening cutoff. “Big” means within the club's full-moon window, which is currently four days on either side of full.
  • Moonrise happens between sunset and the evening cutoff. The moon may be small; the timing is what counts.

All times are Cobb local time (Pacific).

How the Club Counts a Good Night

_□×

This is a very scientific almanac, according to the two club members who own a telescope and the one who owns a clipboard.

Sunset
The moment the top of the sun drops below the horizon. The chart uses the standard definition, including the usual atmospheric refraction.
Big moon
Any night whose Cobb local date falls inside the configured full-moon window. The default is four days before through four days after the full moon, because that is when the moon is still doing its best work.
Above the horizon
The moon's apparent center is at or above the configured altitude. The default is −0.25°, which is roughly when the upper limb clears the horizon. Raise the number if you want the moon safely clear of trees, ridges, or the neighbor's ambitious flagpole.
Moonrise
The moment the top of the moon appears above the horizon. If moonrise lands between sunset and the evening cutoff, the night qualifies even if the moon is not especially big.
Evening cutoff
The end of the useful moonlit window. It is currently 10:00 PM Cobb local time, which is also the club's traditional time to remember that the trail back is uphill.

The chart is calculated for the village of Cobb (38.8222° N, 122.7231° W, about 802 m above sea level). The astronomy comes from Astronomy Engine and is usually within a minute or two of the U.S. Naval Observatory. Ridges, trees, weather, and the odd bear can still change your actual view.

Trail Notes

  • Bring a headlamp anyway. The moon is a mood, not a trail map.
  • Tell someone where you are going. This is the one piece of advice the club takes seriously.
  • Check closures and private land. A bright moon does not grant access.
  • Turn around while you still have energy. The downhill part is a lie told by the map.
  • No moon is not a failure. It is a good excuse for cocoa.

See you under the moon!

Walk quietly, bring a friend, and leave the trail as you found it. The moon has enough dust.
