# Session 2

Session two was about playing a contract as the declarer. The two other major
aspects of the game (bidding and defending against the contract) took a back
seat.

## Planning as the declarer

After the opening lead has been made and partner's cards (**the dummy**, aka the
table) are laid out, the first things the declarer **must** 🙂 do are:

- thank partner
- count the **top tricks**
- determine whether the top tricks are sufficient for **making the contract**
- if we need more tricks, see where and how we can "develop" them
- **make a plan** for playing the entire deal
- *only then* is it finally time to play a card from the dummy to the first
  trick.

Planning is an important part of playing cards — both as the declarer and as the
defender. It is easier to make and follow a plan as the declarer, because you see
AND control both hands of your line. Defending is harder, which is why we tackle
the declarer's play first.

## Developing tricks

We worked through four **Level 1 - Beginner** deals on
[Bridge Master on bridgebase.com](https://www.bridgebase.com/v3/anon/bridgemaster).
Each one taught a way of developing tricks:

- **A-1** (see [Deal A-1](#deal-a-1) below) taught us that in no-trumps, once
  the defense has established tricks in their long suit, we must cash our top
  tricks immediately if they are enough to make the contract, before handing the
  lead back to the defense (or we might not have a chance to collect even our top
  tricks, and be forced to instead discard them, as the defense collects theirs).
- **A-2** taught us how to develop tricks from a sequence of high cards:
  ♣KQJT opposite ♣5432 gives us three tricks once we let the defenders' ace
  win one.
- **A-3** taught us how to win extra tricks by ruffing (trumping) low cards in a
  side suit. Note that these ruffs only win *additional* tricks if we use the
  trump cards from our hand (the declarer or the dummy) that holds the
  *shorter* trump suit of the two.
- **A-4** taught us the maneuver called a **finesse**. Basic example:
  if we have ♠AQ and the opponent *before* us has ♠K2, we can "finesse that king"
  by leading spades from partner's hand and playing ♠Q if the opponent plays low before us.

We will continue with deals **A-5** onwards from the **Level 1 - Beginner** set
on [Bridge Master](https://www.bridgebase.com/v3/anon/bridgemaster). It is public
and does not even require creating an account, although it does show ads.

We can occasionally feature those deals in our session recaps, like this:

## Deal A-1

You can play through the deal by playing every card from every hand.

You can hide any hand and play the others yourself; the hidden hand plays legal
cards but will NOT play intelligently.

```pbn
[Dealer "N"]
[Deal "N:Q4.K54.A654.KJ54 A65.QJT.987.9876 J32.A32.K32.AQ32 KT987.9876.QJT.T"]
[Declarer "S"]
[Contract "3NT"]
[Auction "N"]
1D Pass 2NT Pass
3NT Pass Pass Pass
```

## Reading the auction

- North opens **1♦** with 13 HCP and four cards in each minor. With 4–4 in the
  minors, the partnership agreement is to open **1♦**.
- South has 14 HCP, a balanced 4–3–3–3 hand, and no four-card major. South
  responds **2NT**, an invitational balanced bid.
- North accepts the invitation with **3NT** because they believe their hand is
  better than the absolute minimum they could have had. North–South have
  **27 HCP** between them.
- South is **the declarer**, not North: South was the first player on the
  North–South side to bid no-trumps. North's 3NT only raised the denomination
  South had already named.
- North–South do have an **eight-card club fit** (KJ54 opposite AQ32), but both
  hands are balanced, so no-trumps is the better place to play.
