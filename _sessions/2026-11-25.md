# Session 3

## Card play techniques

We continued getting familiar with the card play techniques by playing contracts
as the declarer. We solved problems **A-5** through **A-10**
of the **Level 1 - Beginner** set
in [Bridge Master on bridgebase.com](https://www.bridgebase.com/v3/anon/bridgemaster).

We learned

* more types of **finesse**, when our side is missing not one but two key cards,
* the concept of a **marked finesse** (or **proven finesse**), when we know 100% that the finesse will succeed,
* the concept and importance of a **stopper** in no-trumps

## More vocabulary

The links below lead to the
[Glossary of contract bridge terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms)
page on Wikipedia. Feel free to click around, discover more terms, and teach them to your instructor.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| [finesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#finesse) | An attempt to gain power for lower-ranking cards by taking advantage of the favorable position of higher-ranking cards held by the opposition. |
| [to be onside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#onside) | Favorably located, from the point of view of the player taking a finesse. If West holds the ♣K and North the ♣AQ, then from South's point of view the ♣K is onside. |
| [to be offside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#offside) | Unfavorably located, from the point of view of the player taking a finesse. If East holds the ♣K and North the ♣AQ, from South's point of view the ♣K is offside: if the finesse in clubs is taken, it will fail. |
| [tenace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#tenace) | A broken sequence of (often) honor cards, such as ♠AQ or ♦KJ. Holding a tenace in a suit allows (but does NOT force!) a side to finesse. |
| [major tenace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#majortenace) | The highest and the third highest remaining cards in a suit, held in the same hand. For example, the ♠AQ before spades have been played. |
| [minor tenace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#minortenace) | The second-highest and the fourth-highest (or lower) remaining cards in a suit, held in the same hand. For example, the ♠KJ before spades have been played. |
| [marked finesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finesse#Marked_finesse) | A finesse that cannot lose, because the opponents' honor is known to be onside. |
| [to be marked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#marked) | To be known to hold a particular card: "He was marked with the ♥Q." |
| [stopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#stopper) | A high card whose primary function is to prevent the opponents from running a suit in a notrump contract. |
| [control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#control) | A feature of a hand which prevents the defenders from taking too many *immediate* tricks in a specific suit. Aces (or voids in trump contracts) are "first-round" controls, kings (or singletons in trump contracts) are "second-round" controls. |
| [master (card)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#master) | The highest card of a suit that is yet to be played. |
| [lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#lead) | The first card played into a trick. |
| [opening lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_lead) | The first card led by defenders. The dummy is not faced until after the opening lead, which makes the choice of opening lead more difficult than other leads. The opening lead can determine the outcome of the deal. |
| to have the lead | To be entitled to lead to the next trick. Also: to be "on lead", to put [someone] on lead. |
| to transfer the lead | To strategically pass the lead to partner. |
| [to enter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#enter) | To win a trick in the opposite hand, thereby giving it the right to lead to the next trick. |
| [entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entry_(cards)) | A card that allows a particular hand to win a trick that partner or an opponent has led to. Entries are vital to [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#communication). |
| [to show out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_contract_bridge_terms#showout) | To fail to follow suit (having no cards in the trick's suit). |
