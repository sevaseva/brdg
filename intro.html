★ Watch & Learn

_□×

Some people learn bridge by reading. Some learn by doing. And some learn by watching a stranger on the internet say "let's just bid that" and immediately regret it.

Below are the videos the club actually recommends when a new player asks where to start. Press play, take notes, and remember: the pause button is your partner too.

The Basics, in Three Parts

_□×

These three videos explain the basic mechanics: who deals, who bids, what a trick is, and why one player's cards end up face-up on the table. Decent quality, friendly pace, and no prior knowledge required.

House Rules for These Videos

  • Ignore the bidding advice. Especially any sentence that begins "maybe we just…" or ends with "let's just bid that." You will learn a simple, coherent bidding system soon, and you will know what your bids and passes actually mean. "Let's just bid that and see what happens" is a fine way to make friends and a terrible way to make a contract.
  • Watch the card play, but don't copy it. It is fine for seeing how the game fits together, but it is neither especially strong nor especially logical, and it is not explained in much depth. Learn the mechanics here; learn to play intelligently somewhere else.

The World-Class Option

_□×

Andrew Robson is an actual competitive world-class player, and his free Level 1 videos are the closest thing to a bridge master leaning over your shoulder and quietly moving your hand to the right card.

The teaching is precise, direct, and intentional — noticeably less "it depends" than most online bridge content. If you have ever finished a tutorial more confused than when you started, this is the antidote.

Watch Level 1 →

Before you get too serious about the game, you do not need to worry about clicking that link. There are a few free teaser videos there if you really must peek, but the three videos above are plenty for now.

If You Get Serious

His subscription runs about ten dollars a month. If bridge becomes your whole personality, that is a bargain. The lessons are bite-sized and paywalled, so it may be worth asking whether a study buddy can share the subscription. (Read the terms first; the club is not a lawyer, though Dot has opinions.)

Happy watching!
